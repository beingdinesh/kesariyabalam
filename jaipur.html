City Guide

Jaipur — the Pink City,
Rajasthan's grand entrance

Forts on hilltops, a walled old city painted terracotta-pink, and bazaars that have sold the same trades for 300 years. For most travelers, Jaipur is where Rajasthan begins.

Best timeOct – Mar
Ideal stay2–3 days
Nearest airportJaipur (JAI)
From Delhi~5 hrs by road

Why travelers start here

Jaipur was founded in 1727 by Maharaja Sawai Jai Singh II, and it's the only planned city of its kind in India from that era — laid out on a grid, with the old town still painted the same terracotta-pink it was given in 1876 to welcome the Prince of Wales. That's the "Pink City" most people have seen in photos, but it's a working city, not a museum: the bazaars inside the old walls still sell what they've sold for centuries — block-printed textiles in Bapu Bazaar, gemstones in Johari Bazaar, and brassware in Kishanpole.

It's also the most common entry point into Rajasthan, connected directly to Delhi and Agra, which is why most first-time itineraries start here.

What to see

Amber Fort

11 KM FROM CITY CENTER · 2–3 HOURS · ARRIVE BEFORE 9AM TO BEAT CROWDS

A hilltop fort-palace overlooking Maota Lake, built from pale yellow and pink sandstone. The Sheesh Mahal (Mirror Palace) inside is the single most photographed room in Rajasthan — thousands of mirror fragments set into the ceiling, designed to turn a single candle flame into a thousand points of light.

City Palace

IN THE OLD CITY · 1.5–2 HOURS

Still partly home to Jaipur's royal family, with museum wings showing royal costumes, weapons, and two of the largest silver vessels in the world — reportedly used to carry Ganges water on a trip to England, since the Maharaja wouldn't drink foreign water.

Hawa Mahal

EDGE OF OLD CITY · 30–45 MINUTES

The "Palace of Winds" — a five-story pink facade with 953 small windows, built so the women of the royal household could watch street life below without being seen. Best viewed from a rooftop café across the street around sunrise, when the stone turns a deeper rose.

Jantar Mantar

NEXT TO CITY PALACE · 45 MINUTES

An 18th-century astronomical observatory — a UNESCO World Heritage Site — with the world's largest stone sundial, accurate to within two seconds.

A realistic 2-day plan

Day 1 — The Fort

  • 8:00 AMArrive at Amber Fort before tour buses. Walk up, or take the jeep if you'd rather skip the climb.
  • 11:30 AMPanna Meena ka Kund — a step-well nearby most tourists miss.
  • 1:00 PMLunch in Amber village.
  • 3:00 PMJal Mahal (Water Palace) — photo stop, viewed from the lakeside.
  • EveningRooftop dinner overlooking the old city.

Day 2 — The City

  • 7:00 AMHawa Mahal from a rooftop café at sunrise.
  • 9:00 AMCity Palace and Jantar Mantar.
  • 12:00 PMWalk the bazaars — Bapu Bazaar for textiles, Johari Bazaar for jewelry.
  • EveningChokhi Dhani for a Rajasthani cultural dinner, if you want the folk-dance, folk-music version of the state in one evening.

Frequently asked questions

How many days do I need in Jaipur?

Two full days covers the main sites comfortably — Amber Fort and Jaigarh on one day, City Palace, Hawa Mahal, and the bazaars on the other. Add a third if you want a slower pace or a day trip to Abhaneri stepwell.

What is the best time to visit Jaipur?

October to March offers warm days and cool evenings — the most comfortable window for walking around forts and the old city. April to June is very hot, often above 40°C.

Is Jaipur safe for foreign tourists?

Jaipur is one of the most tourist-friendly cities in Rajasthan. Standard precautions apply — agree on auto-rickshaw fares beforehand, use prepaid taxis from the airport, and avoid unlicensed guides who approach you at monuments.
